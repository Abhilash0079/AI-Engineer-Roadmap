NLP Fundamentals and Text Preprocessing:

1. What is NLP?
Natural Language Processing (NLP) is a field of AI that enables computers to work with human language, such as English or Hindi.

Examples of NLP applications:
Spam detection: Classify emails or messages as spam or legitimate.
Sentiment analysis: Identify whether a review expresses positive, negative, or neutral sentiment.
Chatbots and AI assistants: Process user messages and generate appropriate responses.

2. Why does text preprocessing matter?
Computers do not automatically understand raw text as meaningful language. Before many NLP models can use text, we need to transform it into a suitable representation.

Consider this example:
Original text               The QUICK foxes are running!
After basic normalization   the quick foxes are running

Lowercasing and removing punctuation are two possible preprocessing steps. Whether to apply them depends on the task.

Common preprocessing operations include:
    - Lowercasing: Convert letters to lowercase.
    - Tokenization: Split text into units such as words or subwords.
    - Stop-word handling: Optionally remove frequent words such as “the” or “is.”
    - Stemming: Reduce words to approximate roots.
    - Lemmatization: Reduce words to dictionary forms.
    - Whitespace and punctuation handling: Normalize or remove unwanted characters when appropriate.
Important: preprocessing is not about removing as much text as possible. For example, removing the word “not” could reverse the meaning of “This product is not good.”

3. Core NLP concepts
A. Tokenization     Tokenization breaks text into smaller units called tokens.
Example: 
Input sentence      Python makes text processing easier
Word tokens         Python  makes   text    processing      easier

Modern transformer models often use subword tokenization, which can split an unfamiliar word into smaller pieces instead of treating the entire word as one token.

B. Stemming vs. lemmatization
Aspect	    Stemming	                                    Lemmatization
Method	    Removes or modifies word endings using rules	Uses linguistic information
Result	    May produce a non-dictionary form	            Usually produces a dictionary form
Example	    studies → studi	                                studies → study
Speed	    Generally faster	                            Often more computationally expensive

For another example, the word running may be reduced to run by either method, depending on the algorithm and configuration.

C. Stop words
Stop words are frequent words such as “the,” “is,” and “and.” Some NLP pipelines remove them to reduce the amount of text being processed.

However, stop-word removal is optional. These words can carry useful meaning, especially in sentiment analysis, question answering, and transformer-based models.

D. Text normalization
Normalization makes text more consistent.

Examples include:
    - Converting HELLO to hello.
    - Replacing repeated whitespace with a single space.
    - Standardizing certain punctuation or Unicode characters.
    - Handling URLs, email addresses, or numbers according to the task.
Do not automatically remove numbers, punctuation, or special symbols. They may contain useful information, such as prices, dates, or emotional emphasis.

4. The NLP preprocessing pipeline
        Raw text                Original sentence or document
        Normalization           Clean and standardize where appropriate
        Tokenization            Split into words or subwords
        Optional processing     Stop words, stemming, or lemmatization if suitable
        Text representation     Convert tokens into numerical inputs

This is a general workflow, not a mandatory sequence. In modern NLP, pretrained tokenizers often perform normalization and subword tokenization together. Some models work best with minimal manual preprocessing.


5. Interview questions and answers
Read these carefully; they are part of today's theory checkpoint.

Q1. What is NLP?
    NLP is a branch of AI that enables computers to process, analyze, and generate human language.

Q2. What is tokenization?
    Tokenization divides text into smaller units called tokens, such as words, subwords, or characters.

Q3. What is the difference between stemming and lemmatization?
    Stemming applies rules to reduce words, sometimes producing non-dictionary forms. Lemmatization uses linguistic information to find a dictionary form.

Q4. What are stop words?
    They are frequently occurring words that some text-processing pipelines remove when doing so is appropriate for the task.

Q5. Why is lowercasing used?
    It can make words such as Python and python consistent and reduce vocabulary size. However, it may remove meaningful distinctions in some tasks.

Q6. Should punctuation always be removed?
    No. Punctuation can convey meaning, sentiment, sentence boundaries, or structure. Its treatment depends on the task and model.

Q7. Why shouldn't we remove the word “not” automatically?
    Because it changes meaning. “Good” and “not good” express different sentiments.

Q8. Why do NLP models need numerical text representations?
    Machine-learning models operate on numerical inputs. Text must be represented numerically, for example using token IDs, TF-IDF features, or embeddings.
